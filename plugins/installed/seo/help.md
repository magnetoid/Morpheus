## What it's for
SEO controls how products, categories and journal posts show up in search results and AI answers: titles and descriptions, structured data, the sitemap and robots.txt, 301 redirects, a 404 monitor, which AI crawlers may read the store, and a per-product score with a list of what to fix. It's for whoever manages search visibility.

## Set it up
Fill in your organisation name, social profile links and search-engine verification codes, and choose the [](field:seo.organization_type) on [SEO](page:settings). Turn on [](field:seo.book_structured_data) if you sell books, and review the AI-crawler list there to decide which bots may fetch your pages. While you're still preparing the store, turn on [](field:seo.hide_until_launch) to keep every page out of search until you're ready, then switch it off on launch day.

## Common tasks
- Fix titles and descriptions in bulk from [Bulk meta](page:bulk-meta), or one product at a time
- Re-run the [Audit](page:audit) to see which products score low and why
- Turn a 404 into a redirect, or dismiss it, from [404s](page:not-found)
- Add or edit a [redirect](page:redirects) by hand, or import a batch from a CSV file
- Check [Sitemap](page:sitemap) for how many URLs are published, and trigger a rebuild or a re-ping of search engines
- Add a tracked [keyword](page:keywords) to watch its ranking over time
- Fill in a title or description pattern once on [Templates](page:templates) instead of editing every product

## Good to know
Changing a setting here never rewrites anything already stored on a product — a title you typed yourself always wins over anything this app generates. Hiding the store until launch stops new pages reaching search and AI crawlers, but shoppers can still browse the storefront normally. Linda can read your SEO details — meta tags, 404s, audit scores — freely, but anything that changes the store, such as setting a title, running an audit, creating a redirect, adding links, or changing site settings, always needs your go-ahead first.
