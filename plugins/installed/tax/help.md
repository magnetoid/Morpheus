## What it's for

This app calculates tax on orders from the regions and rates you set up: flat-rate VAT, US-style sales tax, a reduced rate for one kind of product such as books, or several rates that add up, like a federal and a provincial tax. The storefront applies the right rates from the shopper's address.

## Set it up

Add at least one [tax region](page:regions) and give it a rate before tax can be calculated. On the same page, choose a default region for orders whose address matches none of yours, or turn tax off. Tick "Prices include tax" if your prices already hold the tax, as is usual in the EU, and "Charge tax on shipping" if shipping is taxed where you sell.

## Common tasks

- Add a tax region for a country or state and set its rate from the [Tax regions](page:regions) page.
- Charge a reduced or zero rate on some products: add a category (for example Books) under Categories, give it a rate in each region where it differs, then pick the category in the Tax category card on each product's page.
- Mark a product or one of its variants as not taxable with the Taxable box on its page.
- Ask Linda to list your rates, add a category, put a product in a category, or change the tax settings. Each change waits for your yes.

## Good to know

Tax is charged on what the shopper pays for each item after discounts and coupons. Gift cards, points and store credit pay the total afterwards and do not lower the tax.

A product in a category pays that category's rates in a region that has any, and the region's standard rates elsewhere. When a region has more than one standard rate, every one applies, in the order of their priority number; a rate marked compound is charged on the price plus the taxes before it.

With "Prices include tax" on, shoppers pay the prices as shown, and the invoice, the order page and the confirmation email say how much of the total is tax. With it off, tax is added at checkout and listed rate by rate.

Deleting a category deletes its rates too, and its products go back to the standard rates.
