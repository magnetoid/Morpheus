## What it's for
This app is your storefront: the home page, browsing by product and category, search, the cart, checkout, order confirmation, and each customer's own account area for past orders, returns, cancellations, addresses, saved payment methods and store credit. It also carries your journal, a vendor directory and a marketplace landing page if you sell through vendors, and standard pages: About, Contact, Shipping and Returns. Shoppers use it to browse and buy; its content comes from your catalogue, your orders and your customers, and its look from your active theme.

## Set it up
There is nothing to turn on — it runs as soon as your store has active products and a theme. The name shown throughout the storefront comes from your store's name setting.

## Common tasks
- Browse your storefront as a shopper would before a sale or a new theme goes live.
- Write your own Shipping and Returns page from [Pages](app:cms) — it replaces the generic one shown at those addresses.
- Write an introduction for the Products, Vendors or Journal pages, also from Pages.
- Close the shop to shoppers with the Maintenance card in Settings, Storefront.
- Point a customer to their account page for orders, returns and cancellations.

## Good to know
Home, Cart, Checkout, My account and Contact are built into this app, not pages you create yourself — they're listed for reference in Pages. A message sent through the Contact page is saved as a lead when you have [the CRM app](app:crm) turned on. A customer can cancel their own order themselves only before it is dispatched, and a paid order is refunded the same way any other refund is.

Checkout asks up front only for what a delivery needs: email, name, street, city, region, postal code and country. The apartment line and a phone number are one click away. A signed-in customer starts from their saved address.

Product, category and listing pages show a trail back home: Home, the catalogue, the product's category and those above it, then the page itself. Search engines read the same trail. A book sits under its genre page.

As a shopper types in the search box, the dropdown suggests products whose names contain the words typed, and above them the categories and collections whose names match, with their product counts. An empty one is never suggested. Pressing Enter opens the full results page, which also finds related products that don't contain the words.
