## What it's for

This app lets you define markets: groups of countries with their own currency and locale. For each market it works out what every product would cost in that currency, from your store price, an optional percentage adjustment, the exchange rate and your rounding.

## Set it up

Add a market from the [Markets](page:index) page: a name, a code, its countries, a currency and a locale. Converted prices round exactly, to a whole number, or to end in .99. A market in a different currency from your store needs an exchange rate before you can switch it on. While an active market uses a currency other than your store's, the European Central Bank's daily rates are fetched. To use only your own, choose "Only the rates I type" under Exchange rates at the top of [Markets](page:index).

## Common tasks

- Open a market to see its exchange rate, its source and age, and a ten-product price preview.
- Type your own rate for a currency and save it. The daily fetch never replaces a rate you typed; empty the box and save to go back to the daily rate. Without a daily rate to fall back on, yours stays while an active market uses it.
- Press Fetch rates now to update the rates at once.
- A currency with no rate yet: save the market switched off, set its rate, then switch it on.
- Mark one market as the default, used when a visitor's country matches no other. Switch a market off without deleting it.
- Ask Linda to show markets and rates, change a market, fix a product's price in one or set a rate; she asks for your yes first.

## Good to know

There is no box on the Markets page for a product's price in a market. Ask Linda to fix one or type it in the product's own list of prices; a fixed price is in the market's currency, is never rounded, and is removed if the market changes currency. Either wins over the conversion, and the ten-product preview on a market's page shows the result. A rate that is more than three days old is flagged as out of date. Shoppers still see your store currency: showing and charging a market's prices comes in a later update, so a market changes nothing a shopper sees yet apart from the page's declared language.
